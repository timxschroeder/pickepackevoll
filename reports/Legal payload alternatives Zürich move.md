# Swap the box van, skip the border

The border split has no legal gap at the border. Its flaw is everything before the border. Michelle may drive a Swiss rental into Germany and back. But the Crafter is still overloaded for about 800 km of German motorway before the split. So the plan never makes the whole trip legal. The overload is also bigger than first thought. The 815 kg rated payload already includes a 75 kg driver but not the passenger. With Michelle in the cab, the van really carries only **about 730–750 kg of cargo**. Against an 850–930 kg load, that is **100–200 kg over, or roughly 3–6 % of 3,500 kg**, which can cross Germany's 5 % fine threshold. The cheapest legal fix with the least effort is to **swap the booked box van for a long high-roof panel van with ≥1,100 kg payload at the same rental company**. The project's packer fits the whole load into a Sprinter long or extra-long high roof, except the two bikes, and those can stand upright at the door or travel by train. If the swap fails, the fallback is to send Michelle by train with one bike and luggage and to post a few boxes after crossing. That removes enough weight for a legal single-van trip. The border split should only be a last resort.

## The border split works at the border but fails on the 800 km before it

On the customs question, the plan holds. **Art. 215(2a) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2446** gives relief to an EU resident who uses a road vehicle rented for private use under a written contract from a professional rental company. There is no purpose restriction ([Art. 215, gesetze.legal](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2015_2446/215)). **Art. 218(4)** requires the vehicle to leave the EU again within **8 days** ([Art. 218, gesetze.legal](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2015_2446/218)). A same-day hop from Basel to Weil am Rhein and back fits easily. Europcar CH also lets its vans go to Germany with no border fee ([Europcar CH](https://www.europcar.ch/de-ch/p/nutzfahrzeug-mieten)).

The problem is the route. The van is overloaded from Hamburg to Weil am Rhein, and the split fixes only the last 85 km in Switzerland. The plan also stacks up practical risks. Household goods can only be cleared during commercial-goods hours, at Basel/Weil-Autobahn **Mon–Fri 07:00–17:30, Sat 07:00–13:00** (still to be confirmed) ([BAZG address list](https://bazg.admin.ch/dam/bazg/it/dokumente/stab/news/extern_adresse-zollstellen-alle-sprachen-def.pdf.download.pdf/extern_adresse-zollstellen-alle-sprachen-def.pdf)). Europcar CH offers van one-way rentals **only Monday to Thursday** ([Europcar CH](https://www.europcar.ch/de-ch/p/nutzfahrzeug-mieten)). No BAZG source confirms that two vehicles can go on one form 18.44. The whole sequence adds an estimated **3.5–5 hours**.

The payload definition is what turns a small overload into a real one. Under EU 1230/2012, "mass in running order" includes the driver but excludes passengers ([VO (EU) 1230/2012 Art. 2](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2012_1230/2)). Manufacturers subtract 75 kg per passenger seat from the payload ([Carthago weight notes](https://www.carthago.com/fileadmin/user_upload/lModelljahr2023/PDFs_2023/Rechtliche_Hinweise_07_23/CA_Katalogbeileger-Gewicht_Einzelseiten_DE_V3.1.pdf)). So 815 kg minus Michelle and minus any driver weight above 75 kg leaves about 730–750 kg. Germany's fine catalogue starts at **€10 above 5 %** and has no bracket at or below 5 % ([BKatV Anhang](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bkatv_2013/anhang_2.html)). The fine itself is trivial. The bigger risk is that insurers can treat overloading as gross negligence after an accident ([ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)). Switzerland can file a criminal report above 5 % ([Blick](https://www.blick.ch/auto/service/ratgeber-mit-dem-wohnmobil-ins-ausland-uebergewicht-kann-teuer-werden-id17672507.html)). Production tolerance on kerb weight is ±5 % ([Carthago](https://www.carthago.com/fileadmin/user_upload/lModelljahr2023/PDFs_2023/Rechtliche_Hinweise_07_23/CA_Katalogbeileger-Gewicht_Einzelseiten_DE_V3.1.pdf)), so only a weighbridge gives the real answer.

## A panel van carries 300–400 kg more because it has no box and no lift

The booked van is a normal unit for its class. Hertz Vans CH lists its tail-lift box van at **815 kg**, while its long panel van carries **1,100 kg** ([hertzvans.ch](https://hertzvans.ch/de/flotte/)). The box body and the tail lift take the difference; a 3.5 t tail lift alone weighs about **180 kg** ([eurotransport.de](https://www.eurotransport.de/fahrzeuge/lkw/dautel-neue-ladebordwand-fuer-3-5-bis-5-5-tonner/)). Dealer listings show **1,196 kg** for a Sprinter 315 long high roof, **1,215 kg** for a Sprinter 317 extra-long high roof and **1,114 kg** for a Crafter L3H3 ([truckscout24](https://www.truckscout24.de/tsp/ts-227-50-392)). Sixt's MAN TGE long is listed at only **950 kg** ([Sixt](https://www.sixt.de/lkw/transporter-einwegmiete)).

The coordinator ran the project's 3D packer on these vans. The **MAN TGE long** (421×174×179 cm) fits only 62 of 73 pieces. The **Sprinter long high roof** (407×187×193 cm) and the **extra-long high roof** (470×178×193 cm) fit everything except the two bikes. The extra-long also leaves 55 cm free at the door. The bikes fail only because the model lays them flat on top of a 170 cm stack of boxes. In a van 193 cm high they can stand upright in the door gap. At about 1,100–1,200 kg payload, the real cargo capacity with Michelle aboard is still about **1,000–1,100 kg**. That covers the 927 kg modelled load with around 100 kg to spare. Rentals guarantee a class, not a model. So the swap is only safe once the station confirms the payload of the actual vehicle: field F.2 minus field G on the registration certificate.

The panel van has two drawbacks. It has **no tail lift**, so heavy pieces (the cabinet base, the box-spring bases) must be lifted in by hand. And the load space is tight, so pack it with the planner's layout. Price is not a drawback. The panel van's list rate is the same or lower (Hertz CH: C4 from CHF 89 per day versus H4 from CHF 109 ([hertzvans.ch](https://hertzvans.ch/de/flotte/))). Swapping at the same company also keeps whatever one-way arrangement to Zürich is already booked. International van one-ways are otherwise hard to get, and Hertz CH quotes them from **CHF 900** ([hertzvans.ch](https://hertzvans.ch/)).

## Ranking the options for cost, effort and legality

| Rank | Option | Weight it fixes | Cost | Effort | Legal whole trip? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Swap to a Sprinter/Crafter long or extra-long high roof, ≥1,100 kg, same company | +300–400 kg capacity | ≈ same rate | One phone call; load by hand | **Yes**, if the confirmed payload is ≥1,100 kg |
| 2 | Michelle by train with 1 bike + 3 bags | ~120–135 kg (her body weight + ~55–70 kg goods) | Ticket + bike reservation | Low; Tim drives 880 km alone | Yes if the load is ≤ ~870 kg; otherwise combine with 3 |
| 3 | Post 5–10 boxes after the border crossing, on a Nachsendungs-list | 100–200 kg | ~€245–490 at 20 kg/parcel + possible Swiss Post fees | Medium | Yes |
| 4 | Leave the glass cabinet (120 kg) behind: sell or give away | 120 kg | Value loss, roughly €200–600 (estimate) | Low–medium | Yes, for the lower load estimate |
| 5 | Second car one-way Hamburg → Zürich (or own car if there is one) | 300+ kg | ~€450–1,000 rented; fuel only if own car | Medium | Yes |
| 6 | Beiladung with a removal firm | up to 5 m³ | from ~€800, customs included | Low | Yes |
| 7 | Trailer ≤750 kg | ~100–150 kg net | Buy, register, insure; no one-way rental exists | High | Yes, but poor value |
| 8 | Border split at Basel | fixes the Swiss leg only | Swiss van rental + 3.5–5 h | High | **No**, overloaded in Germany |

The train option works because a passenger who leaves the cab takes her own weight out of the payload too. On DB international trips a passenger may take up to three easily carried bags, plus one bike with a mandatory reservation ([bahn.de](https://www.bahn.de/service/zug/gepaeck); [SBB](https://www.sbb.ch/de/reiseinformationen/individuelle-beduerfnisse/reisen-mit-velos/mit-dem-velo-nach-europa.html)). Only one bike is allowed per passenger. With Tim alone in the cab, the box van carries about 805 kg of cargo. Taking ~70 kg off by train brings an 850 kg load under that, but a 930 kg load stays about 50 kg over. That gap needs a few parcels. How a train passenger declares household goods at the border is not confirmed.

For parcels, DHL is effectively the only option for private senders to Switzerland, at **€48.99 per 20 kg** or **€62.99 per 30 kg** ([DHL](https://www.dhl.de/en/privatkunden/pakete-versenden/weltweit-versenden/preise-international.html); [paketda.de](https://www.paketda.de/ausland/schweiz.html)). The order matters. Later shipments must be declared on a separate list at the **first import** ([BAZG Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf); [BAZG FAQ](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)). So the van crosses first and the parcels are sent afterwards. Swiss Post normally charges **CHF 13 plus 3 % of value** per parcel ([post.ch](https://www.post.ch/en/receiving-mail/import-customs-clearance)). Whether it waives that for declared household goods is not known.

A rented second car going one-way into Switzerland costs a lot, because the car cannot be re-rented there. Fees start at **250+ €** ([mietwagen-radar.de](https://www.mietwagen-radar.de/einwegmiete-mietwagen/)). A Hamburg removal firm quotes Beiladung to Zürich from about **€800** with form 18.44 handled ([Fuhrmann Umzüge](https://fuhrmann-umzuege.de/umzugskosten-schweiz/)). The trailer option fails on logistics, not on licensing. Class B allows a trailer ≤750 kg behind a 3.5 t van ([FeV §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__6.html)). But nobody offers a one-way trailer rental to Switzerland ([mietwagen-talk](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/4454-anh-nger-einwegmieten/)). A trailer bought just before the move also does not qualify as household goods ([BAZG](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/umzug-uebersiedlungsgut)).

## Recommended plan and the calls that decide it

Call the rental company first. Swap the box van for a **Sprinter or Crafter long or extra-long high roof** with a confirmed payload of **≥1,100 kg**, on the same one-way terms to Zürich. Pack with the planner and stand the bikes upright at the door. Before leaving, weigh the loaded van with both of you aboard and a full tank. The target is ≤3,500 kg total, and check axle loads if the scale can measure them. Then drive one vehicle to Basel/Weil-Autobahn on a weekday and clear it on one form 18.44.

If the swap is not possible or the confirmed payload is below about 1,050 kg, keep the box van. Michelle takes the train with one bike and three bags. Leave out the glass cabinet or 5–10 boxes, and post the boxes after crossing, listed on the Nachsendungs-list. Weigh again in Hamburg. Use the border split only if all of this fails, and accept that it does not make the German leg legal.

The calls are short. **Rental station:** the exact vehicle's F.2 and G values (payload), one-way to Zürich allowed, cross-border option. **HME Billbrook weighbridge**, 040-73 43 36-0, Mon–Fri 06:00–17:30 and Sat 08:00–14:00, price not published ([HME](https://hme-hamburg.de/was-wir-machen/verwiegungen)). The alternative is ADAC Hansa, 040 23 91 91 70, €29/39 by appointment, height limit 3.40 m. The ~3.45 m box van will not fit, but a panel van will ([ADAC Hansa](https://www.adac.de/der-adac/regionalclubs/hansa/mobilitaet-sicherheit/wiegen-wohnmobilen/)). **BAZG**: confirm Weil-Autobahn's hours and the lane for vans, and how goods carried by train passengers and later parcels go on the Nachsendungs-list. Call +41 58 467 15 15 or Weil-Autobahn on +41 58 466 11 11. If you post boxes, ask **Swiss Post customs** (+41 848 48 48 47) whether its fee applies to parcels of declared household goods.

## Conclusion

The question was where to split the load. The better question is which van to rent. A tail-lift box van spends 300–400 kg of its 3.5 t on body and lift. A panel van turns that weight into payload and still fits this load. The move's weight problem comes mostly from the vehicle choice and the passenger rule, not from too many belongings. The bed's disputed weight matters only on the box van, because a ≥1,100 kg panel van covers even the top estimate. One open risk remains on the panel-van route: the actual vehicle's payload. One phone call settles it, and one weighbridge visit confirms it.
