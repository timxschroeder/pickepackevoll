# Split the load at Basel, on a weekday

Basel works well as the place to split the load. Use the motorway customs office **Basel/Weil am Rhein-Autobahn** on a **Monday to Thursday**. Michelle takes the S-Bahn from Weil am Rhein or Lörrach to Basel Badischer Bahnhof and rents a Europcar van a few minutes' walk away. Europcar's Swiss terms allow vans into Germany with no surcharge and allow one-way returns to Zürich, but only Monday to Thursday. Both vans then cross together and clear one form 18.44 with one inventory. Option A (a Swiss helper drives in convoy and clears with you) is legally simpler, because it avoids every question about an EU resident driving a Swiss rental. If a helper is available, it is the better choice. The bigger point is that **neither option fixes the actual legal problem**. The van is overloaded for the whole ~800 km through Germany, not at the border. At 850–930 kg of load against an 815 kg payload, the excess is 35–115 kg. That is **about 1–3 % over the 3,500 kg gross weight**. This is below Germany's first fine bracket (>5 %), but it is still an offence, and it gives insurers and the rental company grounds to refuse cover after an accident. Splitting at the border only helps on Swiss roads. The cheapest real fix is to take 115+ kg out of the Crafter in Hamburg.

## A 1–3 % overload is unfined in Germany but still an offence

Germany treats overloading a vehicle up to 7.5 t as an administrative offence for both the driver and the keeper. The fine catalogue starts at **€10 for more than 5 %** and rises to €30 (>10 %), €95 plus one point (>20 %) and €235 (>30 %) ([BKatV Anhang, Tabelle 3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bkatv_2013/anhang_2.html); [BKatV Anlage Nr. 198.2/199.2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bkatv_2013/anlage.html)). The catalogue has **no bracket at or below 5 %**. That is why ARAG calls up to 5 % "toleriert" for vehicles ≤7.5 t ([ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)). This tolerance is how the rules are enforced in practice, not a legal permission. Gross weight and **each axle load** are limits in their own right, so a van at 101 % gross with a heavy rear axle can break the axle limit by more. Police weigh gross weight and axle loads with mobile wheel-load scales. In the worst case they stop the journey: on the A9 in January 2025 police made a driver unload a van running at about 6 t into a second van before letting him continue ([transport-online.de/dpa](https://transport-online.de/news/ueberladen-polizei-zieht-transporter-aus-dem-verkehr-176370.html)).

For you, the fine is not the real risk. The real risk is what happens after an accident. Insurers can treat overloading as **gross negligence** and reduce or refuse payment ([ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)). Rental contracts usually forbid exceeding the gross weight, and their damage waivers usually exclude contract breaches (an inference; check your actual contract). Switzerland is stricter in principle. It issues fixed fines of CHF 100–250, and above 5 % over it can file a criminal report ([Blick](https://www.blick.ch/auto/service/ratgeber-mit-dem-wohnmobil-ins-ausland-uebergewicht-kann-teuer-werden-id17672507.html)). Swiss case law also holds that the 5 % tolerance must not be "planned in" ([Obergericht Uri 1999](https://entscheidsuche.ch/docs/UR_Gerichte/UR_REB_001_98-99-16_1999-07-15.pdf)). At 1–3 % you are inside both countries' fine thresholds. But you would be deliberately planning to drive overloaded, which is exactly what that Swiss ruling warns against.

This changes what the border split is for. A split near Basel makes the last 85 km in Switzerland legal and gives you a clean customs stop. It **does nothing for the German leg**, which is where nearly all the driving and nearly all the risk are. The only way to make the whole trip legal is to start the trip under 3,500 kg. Weigh the loaded Crafter on a public truck scale before you leave Hamburg (about €5–10, [bussgeldkatalog.org](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.org/ueberladung/)). If you are over, take the heaviest dense items (books, tools, boxed kitchenware) out before you set off.

## Basel beats the other crossings on rentals, customs hours and route

All the crossings share one hard rule. Household goods are cleared only at offices that handle commercial goods, during those offices' hours: Monday to Friday, and Saturday morning at some large offices ([BAZG FAQ Umzugsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)). That rules out minor crossings. After that, it comes down to whether the office opens on Saturday and whether a van can be rented within walking distance.

Basel wins for three reasons. First, it sits on the A5, the direct route from Hamburg, so there is no detour. Second, Basel Badischer Bahnhof (Schwarzwaldallee 200) is one stop from Weil am Rhein or Lörrach ([bahnhof.de](https://www.bahnhof.de/basel-bad-bf/mobilitaetsservice)), and tram 8 also runs from Weil am Rhein into Basel about every 15 minutes ([search.ch](https://search.ch/fahrplan/Basel,Neubad)). Third, it has the longest Saturday clearance window of any candidate. Europcar Basel rents vans from 2 to 20 m³ in the city centre, at the station and at the airport ([Europcar Basel](https://www.europcar.be/en-be/places/van-rental-switzerland/basel)). Third-party listings put both Europcar and Sixt at Schwarzwaldallee 242, right next to the Badischer Bahnhof. Europcar's desk there is listed as open Mon–Sat 07:30–18:30 ([Auto Europe](https://www.autoeurope.ca/car-rental-basel-sbb-railway-station/)). These listings are unverified and may mix up the station names, so phone to confirm. Avoid EuroAirport. It is on French soil on the wrong side of Basel, and a car returned on the French side can trigger repatriation fees ([Enterprise CH](https://www.enterprise.ch/en/support/arrival-/-return-directions-basel-airport-)). Thayngen is only better if you take the A7/A81 via Stuttgart. In that case Hertz Schaffhausen (vans, Sat 08–12) and Avis Schaffhausen (Sat 07–12) fit the same plan.

## Option B in Basel: a weekday sequence of about four hours

Choose the rental company first, because its terms decide everything else. **Europcar CH** explicitly allows its vans to be driven abroad as long as they come back to a Swiss station. There is no border fee for Germany (the fee applies only to HR, CZ, HU, SK and SI). A category B licence is enough for every van in its fleet. It offers one-way rentals between Swiss stations **only Monday to Thursday** ([Europcar CH Nutzfahrzeug mieten](https://www.europcar.ch/de-ch/p/nutzfahrzeug-mieten)). **Sixt** requires you to book a paid "Auslandsfahrt" option, and without it all insurance is void. Sixt offers one-way for vans for a fee shown at booking ([Sixt CH help](https://www.sixt.ch/help-center/articles/mit-dem-sixt-mietwagen-aus-der-schweiz-ins-ausland-welche-laender-sind-erlaubt/); [Sixt CH Lieferwagen Basel](https://www.sixt.ch/lkw/schweiz/basel/)). **Avis** also needs a cross-border add-on. It advertises no one-way fee between Basel and Zürich, possibly for cars only ([Avis CH](https://www.avis.ch/rund-um-avis/extras/cross-border-fee); [Avis Einwegmiete](https://www.avis.ch/rund-um-avis/serviceleistungen/einwegmiete)). **Mobility is out.** Its terms forbid customers living outside Switzerland from driving abroad, and every car must go back to its original location ([Mobility AGB §21e](https://www.mobility.ch/de/agb); [Mobility FAQ](https://www.mobility.ch/de/faq)).

Even the smallest van carries the 100–300 kg you might move: a VW Caddy Maxi takes 621 kg and a T6 takes 904 kg ([Europcar CH](https://www.europcar.ch/de-ch/p/nutzfahrzeug-mieten)). Europcar includes 150 km per day, which covers Basel → Weil → Zürich (about 100 km) comfortably. The research found no confirmed CHF prices, because the booking engines could not be read. Get a live quote. A Rheinfelden-area broker advertises moving vans from CHF 140 including 150 km ([transportero.ch](https://transportero.ch/aargau/rheinfelden/)), which gives a rough idea of the price level. Expect to need a credit card in the driver's name; Europcar Basel does not accept debit or prepaid cards ([Europcar Basel](https://www.europcar.be/en-be/places/van-rental-switzerland/basel)).

The sequence below is estimated from the station locations and customs hours. Nobody has timed it. First, Tim parks the Crafter on a large **ground-level** retail lot or near the station in Weil am Rhein or Lörrach, and stays with it. Avoid underground garages: a high-roof Crafter is about 2.6 m tall, and their clearance is usually 2.0–2.2 m. The Rhein Center lot (Hauptstraße 435, Mon–Sat 08:00–22:00) is one candidate, but its clearance is not documented ([coparking.ch](https://coparking.ch/en/Germany/parkings/33099)). Michelle takes the S-Bahn or tram to Basel Bad. Bf (20–30 min). She does the rental paperwork (20–30 min) and drives the empty van back across the border to the Crafter (15–25 min). An empty van with nothing to declare can use any crossing. Together they move the excess load (30–45 min). Then **both vans drive in convoy to Basel/Weil-Autobahn** and clear together (30–90 min, queue unknown). Ask at the booth which lane a ≤3.5 t van with household goods should use; this was not verified. From there it is 85 km to Zürich (about 1 h 15 min), and the rental is returned one-way at a Zürich station. Allow **about 3.5–5 hours in total**. On a Saturday the 13:00 customs closing time makes the plan risky. Europcar also gives no one-way on a Saturday, so you would have to return the van to Basel (about 2 extra hours) or pay a Sixt one-way fee.

## Customs: one form 18.44, one inventory, both vans in the same queue

Household goods (Übersiedlungsgut) enter Switzerland free of duty and VAT if you used them yourselves abroad for **at least six months** and will keep using them in Switzerland. Moving your residence to Switzerland is "die wichtigste Bedingung" ([BAZG Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)). You declare them on **form 18.44**. It must be presented in duplicate with original signatures and come with an inventory, proof of your Zürich flat (rental contract), and optionally the employment contract or deregistration certificate. EU/EFTA citizens need no residence permit ([Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)). BAZG provides an inventory template ("Liste Übersiedlungsgut") on its Vorgehen page. The form has fields for co-moving persons, for "Gesamteinfuhr" (complete import) versus "Teileinfuhr" (partial import), and for later shipments. Goods must be imported within **2 years** of the move. If documents are missing, customs can clear provisionally against a deposit ([Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf); [BAZG FAQ](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)). No BAZG page explicitly covers two vehicles on one declaration. The form layout supports it, though: make one complete list with a "Fahrzeug 1 / Fahrzeug 2" section each, sign one form "Gesamteinfuhr", and present both vans at the same moment. Phone the Weil-Autobahn office (+41 58 466 11 11) beforehand to confirm. If the vans have to cross separately, declare the first as a "Teileinfuhr" and list the second load as a later shipment.

**Option A** works within the same framework. The exemption belongs to the people moving in, not to the driver. The form lets an absent owner hand the signed form and documents to a representative ("Beauftragter") ([Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)), so a helper could in principle clear your goods alone. A Swiss resident arriving with a car full of someone else's furniture is likely to be questioned, though. The BAZG FAQ says nothing about friends carrying goods ([BAZG FAQ](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)). The robust version of Option A is therefore the same convoy as Option B: the helper's vehicle is cleared at the same moment, at the same office, on your form. A Swiss-registered car driven into Germany by its Swiss-resident owner is ordinary tourist traffic. Its vignette is already in place, and there is no rental contract to worry about. That is why Option A is simpler than B whenever a helper is available. Keep every vehicle at or below 3.5 t. Above that, LSVA applies and a C1 licence is required ([BAZG overview SVAG/NSAG](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/Iy49tfJEYUez/uebersicht_svag-nsag_de.pdf)).

### Michelle in a Swiss rental in Germany: allowed under the current EU text, but the two research notes disagree

The main legal reading is that this is allowed. Since an amendment to the EU customs rules, **Art. 215(2a) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2446** grants full duty relief to a person resident in the EU who uses a road vehicle rented from a professional rental company under a written contract for private use ([Art. 215, gesetze.legal](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2015_2446/215)). **Art. 218(4)** requires that such a vehicle be re-exported within **8 days** ([Art. 218, gesetze.legal](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2015_2446/218)). A trip of a few kilometres into Germany and back on the same day fits easily. Carry the written rental contract.

**One research note disagrees.** It states, explicitly from memory and unverified, that a stricter rule applies: EU residents may use a third-country hired vehicle *only* to return to their home or to leave the EU. That is also how older guidance from ÖAMTC and the Austrian finance ministry describes it ([ÖAMTC](https://www.oeamtc.at/schutzbrief_info/72/); [BMF Austria](https://www.bmf.gv.at/zoll/fuer-unternehmen/RegelungFirmenfahrzeuge/Firmen-KFZ_in_der_EU.html)). The cited article texts are more specific and more recent, so they take precedence. The stricter reading most likely predates the (2a) amendment. Two cheap steps remove the remaining doubt. Check the current consolidated text on EUR-Lex. Or let whichever of you has **already registered in Zürich** drive the rental, because then the EU rule does not apply at all. The rental company's own foreign-travel terms still apply either way.

## Conclusion

The border split solves a customs-logistics problem, but the real problem is a legal one. Planning Option B adds around four hours, a second rental and a narrow weekday customs window. It moves 35–115 kg of excess, which has been illegal for 800 km before you reach the border. The plan only makes sense if you need the second van for **volume** anyway. If you do, Basel/Weil-Autobahn on a Monday to Thursday with a Europcar one-way van is the cleanest version, and a Swiss helper in convoy is cleaner still. If the issue is only weight, weigh the van in Hamburg and leave 115+ kg behind. Send it as parcels or carry it by train or car. That gives you a legal trip in both countries and a single-vehicle customs clearance, and the split plan becomes unnecessary.

What remains open is mostly phone work. Confirm Weil-Autobahn's hours and which lane to use. Get live van quotes and one-way fees from Europcar and Sixt Basel. Check the parking clearance in Weil am Rhein. Read the actual German rental contract's overloading clause, because that clause, more than the BKatV, decides who pays if something goes wrong while you are 1–3 % over.
