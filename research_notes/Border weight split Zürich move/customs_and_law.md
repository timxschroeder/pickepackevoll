# Customs and law: splitting an overweight move into two vehicles at the DE/CH border

Scope: Tim and Michelle move their residence Hamburg → Zürich in a German-registered 3.5 t rental van that is over its payload. Plan: split the load at the border into (B) a van Michelle rents in Switzerland, or (A) a Swiss-resident helper's own vehicle. Research date: 14 Oct 2026. Sources: official (BAZG, gesetze-im-internet.de) wherever possible; portal/press sources marked.

## 1. Swiss customs for Übersiedlungsgut (household goods on moving), several vehicles, who declares

### Takeaway
Household goods of people moving their residence to Switzerland are duty- and VAT-free if they were used personally for ≥6 months abroad and will continue to be used by the movers. The declaration is form 18.44 (in duplicate, originally signed) plus an inventory list, made on working days during the opening hours for commercial goods. The form allows a partial import ("Teileinfuhr") with later shipments listed separately. Two vehicles crossing together at the same office can almost certainly be cleared as one declaration with one inventory, but no official page says this in so many words. A person who is not present can give a representative the signed form and documents, so one partner can clear goods belonging to both.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis**: Art. 14 Zollverordnung (ZV, SR 631.01) and Art. 8 para. 2 let. c Zollgesetz (ZG, SR 631.0). The relocation of the residence to Switzerland is "die wichtigste Bedingung" (the most important condition). — [BAZG: Umzug in die Schweiz – Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)
- **What counts** (text of Art. 14 ZV, printed on form 18.44): goods of people moving in that they used "während mindestens sechs Monaten im Zollausland" (for at least six months abroad) for personal living or their profession, and that are meant for their own continued use in Switzerland. Household provisions are included, plus alcohol up to 200 l (≤25 % vol) and 12 l (>25 % vol). — [Form 18.44 (PDF, edition 01.2022)](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- **Documents to present with form 18.44** (form notes, "Verfahren" item 2):
  - (a) an inventory of the goods. Later shipments go on a separate list. Goods that do not qualify go at the end of the list as "zu veranlagende Waren" (goods to be assessed for duty).
  - (b) a Swiss residence permit. This is **not required for EU/EFTA nationals**.
  - (c) the foreign registration document for any means of transport being imported.
  - (d) proof of buying or renting a house or flat.

  Customs may also ask for an employment contract, the deregistration certificate from the country of departure, or the registration certificate from the new commune. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- EU/EFTA arrivals can prove the move with an employment contract, a rental contract or a deregistration confirmation. BAZG offers a "Liste Übersiedlungsgut" template for the inventory. — [BAZG Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)
- **Form layout**: section 1 is signed by "der zuziehenden Person" (the person moving in) and has a field "Mitübersiedelnde Personen (Co-immigrating persons)". Section 2 (the customs declaration) is signed by "der anmeldepflichtigen Person" (the declarant) and has tick boxes for **"Gesamteinfuhr" (complete importation)** or **"Teileinfuhr gem. besonderem Verzeichnis" (partial importation as per separate list)**. It also has a field "Nachsendung folgt ungefähr am" (later shipment follows around …), plus fields for number of packages, weight in kg and estimated total value in CHF. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- **Absent persons and representatives** (form notes, item 3): "Personen, die bei der Zollveranlagung nicht anwesend sind, übergeben das unterschriebene Formular … und die Belege … dem Beauftragten zuhanden der Zollstelle." (People who are absent at clearance hand the signed form and documents to their representative for the customs office.) — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- **Time limits** (form notes, items 4–5): goods must be imported in connection with the move, "spätestens innerhalb von 2 Jahren ab Datum der Wohnsitzverlegung" (at the latest within 2 years of the date of the move). Later shipments must in principle be declared at the first import, and at the latest within 2 years. If an obstacle prevents import, the exemption can still be granted within 3 months after the obstacle is gone. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf). The FAQ says the same: "Innerhalb eines Zeitraums von 2 Jahren besteht diesbezüglich kein Problem" (within 2 years there is no problem). Later shipments are listed separately at the first entry, and the next steps are agreed with the office. — [BAZG FAQ Umzugsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)
  - **Correction to the brief:** the official limit is **2 years, not 3**.
- **Opening hours**: clearance "nur an Werktagen während den Öffnungszeiten für die Veranlagung von Handelswaren" (only on working days during the hours for clearing commercial goods). — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf). The FAQ says "nur von Montag bis Freitag während den Öffnungszeiten", adds that some large offices also open Saturday morning, and lists 08:00–11:30 and 13:30–17:00 Mon–Fri. — [BAZG FAQ Umzugsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut). The Vorgehen page says clearance during commercial-goods opening hours is "unbedingt" (absolutely) required. — [BAZG Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)
- **Optional pre-clearance**: you can send the dossier at least 2 working days ahead, but only to three Ticino offices (Stabio, Vedeggio, Chiasso). None of these is on a Hamburg → Zürich route. — [BAZG Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)
- **Doubts, provisional clearance, misuse**: if customs doubts the entitlement, it can clear provisionally against a security deposit for duties and set a deadline for missing proof. Anyone who obtains the exemption without meeting the conditions commits an offence (item 10). — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- **Form copies**: a photocopied or downloaded form is accepted if it carries an original signature and is presented in duplicate. The "Vordokumente" field is filled in at the office, because local practice varies. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf); [BAZG FAQ](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)
- **Contact**: BAZG information centre +41 58 467 15 15. — [BAZG Vorgehen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/vorgehen-umzug-in-die-schweiz)

### Inferences
- **Duty and VAT**: qualifying Übersiedlungsgut is fully free of duty and import VAT. Only items that do not qualify (new or used for less than 6 months, gifts for others, goods for resale) are assessed at the end of the list. Swiss import VAT is 8.1 % (the rate cited in a forum source [swisslegal/forum reference via search]; not verified on an official page here).
- **Two vehicles at the same time**: form 18.44 is per declaration, and its "Zeichen, Nr., Anzahl, Art der Packstücke" (packages) field covers any number of packages. Two vehicles presenting together at the same office, with one inventory and one form signed by both partners (or one form plus "Mitübersiedelnde Personen"), should be clearable as one "Gesamteinfuhr". No official page explicitly addresses "two vehicles, one declaration". **Ask the chosen office beforehand.**
- **Later separate crossing**: if the second vehicle crosses at a different time or office, declare it as a "Teileinfuhr", with the second vehicle's goods on a separate list as a "Nachsendung" (later shipment). The first clearance's document number is then referenced as "Vordokument" (previous document).
- **One partner declaring for both**: Michelle can declare the goods of both. List Tim as a "mitübersiedelnde Person" (co-mover), or have Tim sign section 1 in advance and give her the documents, which the form notes explicitly allow.
- **Practical**: use a large commercial-goods office (e.g. Basel/Weil am Rhein autobahn, Rheinfelden, Schaffhausen/Thayngen, Kreuzlingen) on a weekday, not a small crossing or a weekend. Office hours were not verified per office. Check BAZG "Öffnungszeiten + Adressen".
- **German side**: private household goods leaving the EU need no export declaration, and there is no VAT refund unless goods were bought tax-free for export. **No official source was fetched for this**, so it is listed under Gaps.

### Gaps
- No official BAZG text on whether goods in two simultaneous vehicles go on one declaration. This is inferred from the form layout only.
- Exact commercial-goods opening hours of the specific offices on the Zürich route were not checked.
- German export side (Zoll.de, private household goods leaving the EU) was not checked against an official page. It is generally understood that there is no formality for used private goods, but this is unverified here.
- The 8.1 % VAT figure comes from a secondary search result, not a fetched official page.

## 2. Option A: a Swiss-resident helper carries part of the goods

### Takeaway
The exemption belongs to the person moving in, not to the driver. Form 18.44 explicitly allows an absent owner to hand the signed form and documents to a representative ("Beauftragter"). So a helper can in principle clear the goods with the owners' signed form, proofs and inventory. The **far safer practice is to cross in convoy and clear everything together**, with Tim or Michelle present as declarant. Then nothing ever looks like a Swiss resident importing their own or someone else's goods.

### Cited Findings
- The relief is for "Waren von Zuziehenden … zur eigenen Weiterbenutzung" (goods of the people moving in, for their own continued use). The signer of section 1 is "die zuziehende Person" (the person moving in). — [Form 18.44 / Art. 14 ZV](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- Absent persons may hand the signed form and documents "dem Beauftragten zuhanden der Zollstelle" (to their representative, for the customs office). This is the official basis for someone else presenting the goods. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf); for moving companies the same is described on [ch.ch Umzug in die Schweiz](https://ch.ch/de/zoll/umzug-in-die-schweiz) (hand the documents to the moving company).
- The BAZG FAQ does not cover friends or third parties transporting goods (checked). — [BAZG FAQ Umzugsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)
- Obtaining the exemption without meeting the conditions is an offence. — [Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)

### Inferences
- **If the helper crosses alone**, they should carry:
  - form 18.44 signed by Tim and/or Michelle (in duplicate);
  - the inventory list for that vehicle's goods;
  - copies of the Zürich rental contract and the employment contract or registration;
  - a short signed authorisation (Vollmacht) naming the helper.

  The form itself requires no formal power of attorney, but carrying one is cheap insurance.
- **Risk for an unaccompanied helper**: a Swiss resident arriving with a car full of furniture, without the owners, may be questioned. If the documents are incomplete or the office is closed (weekend or evening, when only passenger-traffic or unstaffed crossings are open), the helper may face provisional assessment with a deposit, or be sent back. A private, unpaid favour is not commercial transport. Being paid for the trip could raise commercial-transport questions; this is not researched.
- **Best practice**: cross in convoy at one staffed commercial-goods office during office hours. Present one form with one inventory (split into "vehicle 1 / vehicle 2" sections) and have the declarant (Tim or Michelle) present. This reduces Option A to the same situation as Option B.

### Gaps
- No official statement found on a "friend as unpaid carrier" specifically, or on whether customs insists on owner presence in practice. Phone confirmation with BAZG (+41 58 467 15 15) or the chosen office is recommended.

## 3. German overloading law for a 3.5 t van (plus Swiss rules at entry)

### Takeaway
In Germany, overloading a vehicle up to 7.5 t is an administrative offence under § 34 StVZO / § 31d StVZO (BKatV Nr. 198.2 for the driver, 199.2 for the keeper). Fines are €10 (>5 %) up to €235 + 1 point (>30 %). There is no fine bracket for ≤5 %, which is the de-facto tolerance, not a legal right. The bigger practical risks are:
- police stopping the journey until the excess is unloaded;
- insurers treating an overload-related accident as gross negligence;
- breach of the rental contract.

Switzerland fines lightly up to 5 % over; above 5 %, a criminal report (Anzeige) is possible.

### Cited Findings
- **BKatV, official text**: Nr. 198 (driver: vehicle put into operation although the permitted axle load or gross weight is exceeded; § 34 Abs. 3 Satz 3, § 31d Abs. 1, § 42, § 69a Abs. 3 Nr. 4 StVZO). Nr. 198.2 covers "andere Kraftfahrzeuge bis 7,5 t". Nr. 199 / 199.2 covers the keeper ("Halter") who orders or permits it (§ 31 Abs. 2 i.V.m. § 34 Abs. 3 Satz 3 StVZO, § 69a Abs. 5 Nr. 3). — [BKatV Anlage](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bkatv_2013/anlage.html)
- **BKatV Tabelle 3, Buchstabe b** (vehicles ≤7.5 t), the same amounts for driver (198.2.x) and keeper (199.2.x): >5 % €10; >10 % €30; >15 % €35; >20 % €95; >25 % €140; >30 % €235. No bracket ≤5 %. — [BKatV Anhang (Tabelle 3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bkatv_2013/anhang_2.html)
- **Points**: 1 point from >20 % (€95 and above). — [bussgeldkatalog.org Überladung (updated 31 May 2026)](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.org/ueberladung/); [ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)
  - Points follow from the Fahreignungsregister rules for fines ≥€60. That rule is not directly fetched here.
- **Tolerance**: ARAG calls up to 5 % "toleriert" for vehicles ≤7.5 t (trucks >7.5 t: fines from 2 %). bussgeldkatalog.org mentions a "Toleranzgrenze von 5 Prozent" but elsewhere says fines are possible "schon bei 2 Prozent", an internal contradiction. — [ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil); [bussgeldkatalog.org](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.org/ueberladung/)
  - **The authoritative point**: the BKatV has no bracket ≤5 %. It is still technically an offence; it just isn't fined via the catalogue.
- **Axle loads count separately**: both permitted gross weight and axle loads (registration fields 8.1, 8.2) apply. — [ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)
- **Weighing**: police use mobile scales (wheel-load pads) that measure gross weight and axle loads. For self-checks there are public truck scales (about €5–10), and some recycling centres have scales. — [bussgeldkatalog.org](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.org/ueberladung/); [ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)
  - No published measuring-tolerance deduction was found.
- **Onward travel stopped**: on 27 Jan 2025 police on the A9 (Hof) stopped a van at about 6 t instead of 3.5 t (+70 %). The driver had to unload 2.5 t into a second van before continuing, and fine proceedings were opened. — [transport-online.de (dpa)](https://transport-online.de/news/ueberladen-polizei-zieht-transporter-aus-dem-verkehr-176370.html)
- **Insurance**: in an accident, overloading may be treated as grobe Fahrlässigkeit (gross negligence). The insurer may then reduce or refuse payment, leaving a significant share of costs with the driver. Premiums may also rise. — [ARAG](https://www.arag.de/rechtsschutzversicherung/verkehrsrechtsschutz/ueberladung-pkw-lkw-anhaenger-wohnmobil)
- **Swiss side**: Ordnungsbussen (fixed fines) of CHF 100–250 depending on degree and vehicle size. Above 5 % over gross weight, an Anzeige (criminal report) with an individually set penalty is possible. — [Blick, updated 13 Jul 2024](https://www.blick.ch/auto/service/ratgeber-mit-dem-wohnmobil-ins-ausland-uebergewicht-kann-teuer-werden-id17672507.html)
  - Swiss case law (Obergericht Uri 1999, upheld by the Federal Court, on the old VRV Art. 67 Abs. 8): the 5 % tolerance must not be "planned in", and above 5 % the **entire** excess is punished. — [Urteil UR 1999](https://entscheidsuche.ch/docs/UR_Gerichte/UR_REB_001_98-99-16_1999-07-15.pdf)
  - The Beobachter says fines could theoretically reach CHF 10,000 but are usually a few hundred francs. — [Beobachter](https://www.beobachter.ch/strassenverkehr/auto-uberladen-hohe-busse)

### Inferences
- **Reading the fines for this van**: €30–€95 for 10–20 % over is small. The real exposure is:
  1. Police (or Swiss customs at the border, which often has scales at commercial lanes) can order unloading on the spot. With a second vehicle already planned, that is manageable, but on the A7/A5 it would be a disaster.
  2. **Swiss entry above 5 % over** risks a criminal report rather than a fixed fine.
  3. An accident while overloaded: the insurer may claim gross negligence, which can mean recourse against the driver or reduced Kasko payout. Rental companies' deductible waivers usually exclude contract breaches.
- **Rental agreements**: standard German van rental terms forbid exceeding the permitted gross weight and make the renter liable for fines and damages. **Not verified against a specific rental company's terms.** Check the actual contract (e.g. Sixt, Europcar, Starcar terms).
- **Splitting the load before the border**: under-load the German van so it is within its gross weight *for the whole German leg*, not just at the border. The German leg (≈800 km) is where most of the exposure is.

### Gaps
- Specific rental-company T&C clauses on overloading and their insurance consequences were not fetched.
- The exact current Swiss OBV (SR 741.031, Anhang 1) item number and amounts for vehicles ≤3.5 t were not retrieved. The Liechtenstein analogue (2004) lists CHF 200 for >100 kg up to 5 % over ([gesetze.li](https://nextmobile.gesetze.li/chrono/pdf/2004252000)). This is **not** confirmed as current Swiss law.
- No source for a formal police measuring-tolerance deduction in Germany.

## 4. German rental van in Switzerland, LSVA, vignette, and the second vehicle

### Takeaway
A 3.5 t van pays no heavy-vehicle fee (LSVA/PSVA apply only above 3.5 t). It needs the Swiss motorway vignette (sticker or e-vignette) for national roads, as does every other vehicle up to 3.5 t, including a Swiss rental van (normally already vignetted) and the helper's car. **Watch the customs status of foreign-registered vehicles**:
- Swiss customs restricts use of non-cleared foreign vehicles by people **resident in Switzerland**.
- The EU restricts use of non-EU-registered vehicles by people **resident in the EU**.

Both can touch this plan around the moving date. Each is probably fine for a one-way move, but both are worth a call to customs.

### Cited Findings
- **Vignette**: required for all domestic and foreign vehicles with a gross weight up to and including 3.5 t, explicitly including Lieferwagen (vans), on national roads only. It is available as a sticker or as an e-vignette via the official Via portal. — [BAZG overview SVAG/NSAG (PDF)](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/Iy49tfJEYUez/uebersicht_svag-nsag_de.pdf)
  - The price (CHF 40/year) is from prior knowledge and was not re-verified here.
- **LSVA/PSVA**: LSVA applies to goods vehicles above 3.5 t on the whole road network. PSVA applies to certain heavy vehicles above 3.5 t (heavy cars, motorhomes, caravans). Neither applies to a vehicle ≤3.5 t. — [BAZG PSVA page](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/pauschale-schwerverkehrsabgabe-psva-fuer-im-ausland-immatrikulierte-fahrzeuge); [BAZG overview SVAG/NSAG](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/Iy49tfJEYUez/uebersicht_svag-nsag_de.pdf)
- **Swiss residents and foreign vehicles**: the duty-free temporary import of private foreign vehicles (1956 convention) requires that the person importing the vehicle is **not** domiciled in Switzerland. Swiss residents generally may not use non-cleared foreign vehicles. — [swisslegal.ch note on foreign company cars](https://www.swisslegal.ch/documents/news/verzollungs-und-immatrikulationspflicht-von-ausl-dienstfahrzeugen.pdf) (law-firm note, not official). Art. 35 ZV allows customs to permit temporary use in specific cases (ibid.).
  - A forum (not official) describes Swiss residents with foreign rental cars requesting a "Vormerkschein 15.25" at the border, limited to about 8 days. — [mietwagen-talk.de forum](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/19773-wohnsitz-ch-ausl-ndische-mietwagen-innerhalb-der-schweiz-nutzen/)
- **EU side, mirror rule**: under Art. 215 UZK-DA, EU residents may use third-country (e.g. Swiss) registered vehicles only in narrowly defined cases, for example employees of the non-EU owner or hirer. Use beyond this generally creates a duty and import-VAT debt. — [IHK note on temporary use of company cars (PDF)](https://www.ihk.de/blueprint/servlet/resource/blob/5505182/a95bda2ba7c17620904075e21491e44f/voruebergehende-verwendung-von-firmenwagen-data.pdf); [BMF Austria](https://www.bmf.gv.at/zoll/fuer-unternehmen/RegelungFirmenfahrzeuge/Firmen-KFZ_in_der_EU.html)

### Inferences
- **German rental van (Tim/Michelle driving into CH on the moving day)**: they are moving in at that moment, and the van leaves again after unloading. Customs routinely handles this in self-moves. Still, ask the office:
  - whether a Vormerkschein 15.25 (temporary admission note) is needed for the rental van because their Swiss residence starts that day;
  - how soon the van must leave (it should be returned to Germany promptly; one-way return in CH is usually not possible with German rentals).
- **Option B (Swiss rental van driven to the German side by Michelle)**: driving a Swiss-registered rental into the EU is a potential EU customs issue if Michelle is still considered **EU-resident** at that moment. Under UZK-DA, a private EU resident's use of a non-EU-registered vehicle is very limited.
  - From recollection, not verified in this session: UZK-DA Art. 216 allows EU residents to use a third-country **hired** vehicle under a written contract only to return to their place of residence in the EU or to leave the EU. A short trip *into* Germany to collect goods and return to CH may arguably fit "leaving the EU". Hauptzollamt confirmation is recommended.
  - If Michelle has already registered in Zürich (and is non-EU-resident), the EU issue disappears. Swiss customs is no issue for a Swiss-registered vehicle.
  - Also check the Swiss rental contract allows trips into Germany (usually yes, sometimes with a fee).
- **Option A (Swiss helper's own car/van)**: a Swiss-registered vehicle with a Swiss-resident driver entering Germany temporarily for private use is the standard tourist case (UZK temporary admission). No issue is expected. The helper's vehicle has a vignette already if Swiss-registered.
- **Helper or Swiss rental vehicle >3.5 t**: if anyone picks a van above 3.5 t, LSVA applies, along with a category C1 licence and truck rules (speed limits, Sunday/night ban). Keep both vehicles ≤3.5 t.

### Gaps
- UZK-DA Art. 216 (EU residents and third-country hired vehicles) was not fetched. The wording is from recollection and must be verified (EUR-Lex, Reg. (EU) 2015/2446).
- No official BAZG text found on Swiss-resident-to-be people driving a German rental van in on moving day. The forum source on the 15.25 note is unofficial.
- The vignette price was not verified on an official page in this session.
- German export side for private household goods is not verified (see section 1).
