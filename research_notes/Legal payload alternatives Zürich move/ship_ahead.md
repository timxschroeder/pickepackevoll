# Shipping 100–300 kg of household goods separately, Hamburg → Zürich (parcel, pallet, part-load, train)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Prices are as published on the cited pages at that date. "UNVERIFIED" marks things I could not confirm from a primary source.

## 1. Parcel options (DHL Paket international, others) and typical cost for 10–20 boxes

### Takeaway
DHL Paket from Germany to Switzerland (Zone 2) costs EUR 34.99 per 10 kg parcel and EUR 48.99 per 20 kg parcel online. Ten 10 kg boxes cost about EUR 350, twenty cost about EUR 700. Heavier parcels are cheaper per kg (30 kg costs EUR 62.99, about EUR 2.10/kg). For private senders, DHL is in practice the only parcel option: Hermes, GLS and DPD to Switzerland need a business contract.

### Cited Findings
- DHL Paket international places Switzerland in **Zone 2 (Great Britain & Switzerland)**. Online / postal outlet prices: up to 5 kg EUR 26.99 / 29.99; **10 kg EUR 34.99 / 37.99; 20 kg EUR 48.99 / 51.99; 30 kg EUR 62.99 / 65.99**. Max size 120 × 60 × 60 cm, length + girth (L + 2W + 2H) ≤ 300 cm. No 31.5 kg tier is listed for Zone 2; 30 kg is the top tier. — [DHL Preise international](https://www.dhl.de/en/privatkunden/pakete-versenden/weltweit-versenden/preise-international.html)
- The bulky-goods (Sperrgut) surcharge for non-EU zones is EUR 30.00 per parcel. The same page says the service is "not available for all countries" and does not confirm that it is offered for Switzerland. — [DHL Preise international](https://www.dhl.de/en/privatkunden/pakete-versenden/weltweit-versenden/preise-international.html)
- DHL Päckchen and standard DHL Pakete to Switzerland are usually delivered by Swiss Post, though some are handed to DHL Express. Transit time is typically 4–7 working days. — [paketda.de Schweiz](https://www.paketda.de/ausland/schweiz.html)
- Shipping from Germany with **GLS, DPD and Hermes to Switzerland is possible only with a business customer contract**. GLS and Hermes work with Swiss Post in Switzerland, while DPD has its own network. Customs clearance fees at GLS/DPD "can be significantly higher" than at Swiss Post. — [paketda.de Schweiz](https://www.paketda.de/ausland/schweiz.html)
- A customs declaration (CN 23) is required for parcels from the EU to non-EU countries such as Switzerland. — [Swiss Post: accompanying documents](https://www.post.ch/en/sending-parcels/declaration-and-clearing-through-customs/international-accompanying-documents); restated in [mypaketshop.com](https://www.mypaketshop.com/ratgeber/versandt/pakete-briefe-warensendungen-in-schweiz-schicken/)
- **Swiss Post import clearance fee ("Verzollungsgebühr"):** CHF 13.00 base fee for parcels from the EU, plus a 3% surcharge on the goods value (including shipping and duties). The total is capped at CHF 70, and paying online saves CHF 1.50. A customs correction request costs CHF 40. — [post.ch import customs clearance](https://www.post.ch/en/receiving-mail/import-customs-clearance)
- Import VAT is generally 8.1%, and amounts up to CHF 5 are not collected. Industrial tariffs have largely been abolished since 2024. — [zollrechnerschweiz.com](https://zollrechnerschweiz.com/post-verzollung-gebuehren/) (secondary source)
- DHL Express Switzerland charges CHF 19 for simplified clearance (Economy Select) or a CHF 19 account-handling fee (Worldwide) when no customs account is on file. — [DHL eBilling FAQ CH](https://ebilling.dhl.com/gpp/web/faq/ch/)

### Inferences
- The given box size of 63 × 29 × 33 cm has a length + girth of 187 cm, well within DHL limits.
- **Cost per kg at DHL:** 10 kg tier EUR 3.50/kg, 20 kg EUR 2.45/kg, 30 kg EUR 2.10/kg. Example options:
  - 100 kg as ten 10 kg boxes: **EUR 350**. As five 20 kg parcels: **about EUR 245**.
  - 200 kg as twenty 10 kg boxes: **EUR 700**. As ten 20 kg parcels: **about EUR 490**.
  - Packing two moving boxes as one parcel saves money, but only if the combined dims stay within 120 × 60 × 60 cm. Two boxes side by side measure 63 × 58 × 33 cm, which fits.
- **Swiss Post fee if the parcels are cleared as normal goods:** CHF 13 plus 3% of value per parcel, possibly with VAT on top. For 10 parcels that is roughly CHF 130–200 or more. Whether Swiss Post waives or reduces its fee when the parcels are cleared duty-free as Übersiedlungsgut is UNVERIFIED (see section 6).
- **Realistic total for 10 boxes of 10 kg:** about EUR 350 shipping plus CHF 0–200 clearance.

### Gaps
- No published Swiss Post procedure or fee for **Übersiedlungsgut arriving by parcel** was found (post.ch searched). Before shipping, ask Swiss Post customs (+41 848 48 48 47) whether they accept form 18.44 plus the inventory list attached to the parcel or uploaded online, and what they charge.
- I did not search Hermes/GLS/DPD private tariffs further, since private senders cannot use them for Switzerland according to paketda.
- Whether DHL Sperrgut (bulky goods) to Switzerland is available is unconfirmed (see section 4).

## 2. Pallet / freight shipping Hamburg → Zürich

### Takeaway
No fixed public price exists for a 300 kg Euro pallet from Germany to Switzerland including customs and private delivery. Sources indicate quotes of a few hundred EUR, plus customs fees and surcharges for delivery to a private address. Several platforms exclude private customers. Effort is high: you need pallet packing, ground-level loading, a customs agent and a tail-lift delivery.

### Cited Findings
- Eurosender lists pallet shipping to Switzerland, but its examples are "auf Anfrage" (price on request), and some prices apply only to business accounts. Pallets are collected and delivered at ground level. — [eurosender Palettentransport Schweiz](https://www.eurosender.com/de/palettentransport/schweiz)
- Pamyra lets private customers book without a business registration, with prices including 19% VAT. Prices for Switzerland vary daily, and the pages found do not say whether customs is included. — [pamyra Palettenversand](https://www.pamyra.de/palettenversand/)
- Cargoboard excludes transports for private individuals entirely. — [Cargoboard use cases](https://cargoboard.com/use-cases)
- Clicktrans states an average pallet price of EUR 389 (no Swiss or customs context). One example order, Switzerland → Germany with one pallet, cost EUR 1,190. — [clicktrans Paletten Schweiz](https://clicktrans.de/transport/paletten/schweiz/)
- For pallets to Switzerland, a guide says customs clearance happens at two customs points and a clearance fee is charged that "varies strongly by provider". — [expresslogistik-reinwald.de](https://expresslogistik-reinwald.de/ratgeber/kurierdienst-schweiz/)
- Removal firms quote **EUR 350–550 flat fees for T1/T2 transit customs documents**. — [search summary of moving-firm guides; e.g. umzug-365.de](https://www.umzug-365.de/internationale-umzuege/umzug-in-die-schweiz/) (UNVERIFIED which exact page states this)

### Inferences
- For 100–300 kg, a pallet plus customs agent plus private-address delivery will plausibly cost **EUR 400–900**. This is an estimate, UNVERIFIED. That is no cheaper than parcels for 100–200 kg and needs much more effort.
- A pallet only makes sense near the 300 kg end, or if bikes and boxes go together.

### Gaps
- No concrete online quote for Hamburg → Zürich, 300 kg, private address, customs included. Getting one requires a live quote (Pamyra, Eurosender).

## 3. Moving companies' part-load (Beiladung) and luggage services

### Takeaway
A Hamburg removal firm advertises Beiladung to Zürich **from about EUR 800 for up to 5 m³**, with customs handling (form 18.44) included and delivery in 5–10 working days. For 100–300 kg (roughly 1–2 m³) that is more than DHL parcels for up to ~200 kg, but it includes customs and needs little effort.

### Cited Findings
- Fuhrmann Umzüge (Hamburg):
  - Beiladung on already planned tours up to 5 m³ "ab 750 €". Indicative prices for 5 m³: Basel ~EUR 750, **Zürich ~EUR 800**.
  - A dedicated tour starts at EUR 999.
  - Customs handling is included and form 18.44 is prepared. Delivery takes 5–10 working days with no fixed date.
  - The page is inconsistent: its headline says "Beiladung ab 999 €". — [fuhrmann-umzuege.de/umzugskosten-schweiz](https://fuhrmann-umzuege.de/umzugskosten-schweiz/)
- General transport price Hamburg–Switzerland: EUR 50–80 per m³ (full moves). — [fuhrmann-umzuege.de](https://fuhrmann-umzuege.de/umzugskosten-schweiz/)
- Removal firms quote whole Germany → Zürich moves at EUR 999–2,000 for a mid-size flat, or CHF 1,500–5,000+. These are vendor pages, not neutral. — [umzug-365.de](https://www.umzug-365.de/internationale-umzuege/umzug-in-die-schweiz/), [einwandern-schweiz.ch/zoll](https://einwandern-schweiz.ch/zoll/)

### Inferences
- Beiladung beats parcels on price only above roughly 200–250 kg of boxes. Its real advantage is that the firm handles customs.
- I found no SBB/Swiss Post door-to-door luggage service from Germany (see section 5).

### Gaps
- No Beiladung price per box or per 100 kg was found. A quote from 2–3 firms (Hamburg–Zürich, ~1.5 m³, ~200 kg) is needed.
- International luggage forwarders (e.g. "Send my Bag"-type services) were not researched.

## 4. Bikes by DHL or bike shipping services to Switzerland

### Takeaway
Within Germany, DHL takes boxed bikes as Sperrgut (bulky goods, about EUR 49–56 domestic). For Zone 2 (Switzerland) DHL lists a EUR 30 bulky surcharge on top of the parcel price, but does not confirm Sperrgut is available to Switzerland. A boxed bike also exceeds the normal 300 cm length + girth limit. Expect about **EUR 80–95 per bike if accepted**, which is UNVERIFIED for Switzerland. Taking bikes on the train is simpler (section 5).

### Cited Findings
- DHL international bulky surcharge for Zones 2–8 is EUR 30.00. The service is "not available for all countries"; check dhl.de/laenderinfo. — [DHL Preise international](https://www.dhl.de/en/privatkunden/pakete-versenden/weltweit-versenden/preise-international.html)
- A typical bike box (140 × 80 × 30 cm) has a length + girth of 360 cm, which is the Sperrgut maximum.
- Domestic DHL Sperrgut surcharge: EUR 28.99. Handlebar and pedals must be removed. — [biketrailspfannenstiel.ch (secondary)](https://biketrailspfannenstiel.ch/auskunft/dhl-versand-fahrrad/)
- Another guide claims DHL and Hermes stopped shipping bikes. The sources contradict each other here. — [wandernundmehr.at](https://www.wandernundmehr.at/faq/was-kostet-ein-fahrrad-mit-dhl-zu-versenden)

### Inferences
- If DHL accepts it for Switzerland: a 20 kg parcel at EUR 48.99 plus EUR 30 = **about EUR 79 per bike**, plus Swiss Post clearance. UNVERIFIED.
- Bikes count as Übersiedlungsgut, provided they were used for 6 months or more. The exclusion of "Beförderungsmittel" applies only to the secondary-residence case (form 18.44, point 4).

### Gaps
- Availability of DHL Sperrgut to Switzerland needs checking on dhl.de/laenderinfo.
- EU bike-shipping services (e.g. bike-box couriers) were not researched for lack of tool budget.

## 5. Carrying luggage and bikes by train (DB/SBB)

### Takeaway
If one person travels by train, they can take a bike plus up to three easily carried bags free of charge. That is about **40–60 kg removed from the van for the price of a ticket plus a bike ticket and reservation**. This is the cheapest and least-effort way to shed 50–100 kg. For 100 kg, both would need to travel by train, or the train trip would need to be combined with a few parcels.

### Cited Findings
- **DB luggage rules:**
  - Domestic: hand luggage plus "ein weiteres größeres Gepäckstück" free of charge.
  - International trips: "bis zu drei leicht tragbare Handgepäckstücke".
  - No fixed weight limit, but each item must be carryable by one person alone. Reference rack size is 700 × 500 × 300 mm. — [bahn.de Gepäck](https://www.bahn.de/service/zug/gepaeck)
- **Bikes on DB long-distance trains:** you need a "Fernverkehrs-Fahrradkarte mit Stellplatzreservierung", and each passenger may bring only one bike. — [bahn.de Gepäck](https://www.bahn.de/service/zug/gepaeck)
- Bike-space reservation is mandatory on ICE/EC to and from Germany. Tickets and reservations for bike trips to Germany must be bought from DB, not SBB. Spaces are limited, so book early. — [SBB: mit dem Velo nach Europa](https://www.sbb.ch/de/reiseinformationen/individuelle-beduerfnisse/reisen-mit-velos/mit-dem-velo-nach-europa.html)
- Domestic DB bike reservation costs EUR 7.50–12.90 depending on distance. — [bussgeldkatalog.org (secondary)](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.org/fahrradmitnahme-bahn/)

### Inferences
- Example for one traveller: two suitcases at ~20–23 kg, one backpack (~8 kg) and one bike (~15 kg) remove about **55–70 kg**. Bike ticket plus reservation is probably about EUR 10–25 (UNVERIFIED for the international fare).
- Two bikes need two travellers, since each passenger may take only one bike.
- Goods carried on the train are still Übersiedlungsgut. They should appear on the inventory or Nachsendung list and be declared at the border. Where and how a train passenger declares them (e.g. at Basel Bad Bf) is UNVERIFIED.

### Gaps
- No 2026 price was found for DB's international bike ticket Hamburg → Zürich. Old forum figures (CHF 20 / EUR 9) are outdated.
- The customs procedure for a train passenger carrying Übersiedlungsgut was not confirmed.

## 6. Rules: Übersiedlungsgut shipped separately from the person (Switzerland)

### Takeaway
Goods shipped separately ("Nachsendungen") can still enter duty-free. They must be declared on a **separate list at the first import** (when the van crosses) and imported within **2 years** of the change of residence. They must have been used abroad for at least 6 months. Clearance happens only on working days during commercial-goods hours. Shipping goods ahead of the van (before the first import) is not covered by the rules. Agree it with the customs office in advance.

### Cited Findings
- Form 18.44 rules (Art. 14 ZV), from the official form text:
  - Goods qualify if they have been used for at least 6 months abroad and will continue to be used by the owner. They are duty-free.
  - "Allfällige Nachsendungen sind bei der ersten Einfuhr anzumelden."
  - With the form, submit "ein Verzeichnis der einzuführenden Waren; Waren, die als Nachsendungen eingeführt werden, sind auf einem separaten Verzeichnis anzumelden". Also submit proof of renting or buying a flat. EU citizens need no Swiss residence permit.
  - Import must happen "spätestens innerhalb von 2 Jahren" from the date of the change of residence. Nachsendungen must be declared at the first import, at the latest within 2 years.
  - People not present at clearance give the signed form and documents to their representative.
  - The form must carry an original signature and be submitted in duplicate. Clearance is only on working days during commercial-goods hours. — [BAZG Form 18.44](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/lokVGdOnQG-l/18_44_uebersiedlungsgut_d.pdf)
- BAZG FAQ:
  - Nachsendungen "müssen der Zollstelle bereits bei der ersten Einfuhr auf einer separaten Liste angemeldet werden". The rest of the procedure is to be agreed with the customs office.
  - Duty exemption for later shipments is granted "nur unter bestimmten Voraussetzungen".
  - A 2-year period is unproblematic.
  - Provisional clearance with a deposit is possible if documents are missing (refund minus a fee if documents follow within 6 months). — [BAZG FAQ Umzugsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-umzugsgut)
- A private guide says the dossier can be sent to the customs office at least 2 working days before crossing, as a pre-check. It also says "18 months", which contradicts the official 2 years; the official form wins. — [einwandern-schweiz.ch/zoll](https://einwandern-schweiz.ch/zoll/)
- Swiss postal clearance is generally done ex officio by the post customs office. One exception applies when the recipient asks for clearance on the accompanying documents. — [Zollverordnung via lexfind](https://www.lexfind.ch/tolv/149009/de) (search snippet, UNVERIFIED in full text)

### Inferences
- **Safest order:** cross the border with the van first and declare the van load plus a separate Nachsendungs-list on form 18.44 (boxes, bikes). Then send the parcels **after** the move, addressed to the new Zürich address. Swiss Post then clears them against the stamped 18.44 / Nachsendungs-list.
  - Sending parcels **ahead** of the van is legally murky. Before the first import there is no 18.44 to refer to, and Swiss Post may clear them as ordinary goods (CHF 13 + 3% value + VAT).
  - Sending ahead might work by asking Swiss Post for clearance on accompanying documents and enclosing 18.44 plus the inventory (in a document pouch marked "Umzugsgut / Übersiedlungsgut"), but this is UNVERIFIED.
- In practice, low declared values (used household goods) keep the 3% surcharge and VAT small. Even "worst case" ordinary clearance of ten parcels with ~CHF 50 value each is roughly CHF 13–15 per parcel, about CHF 150 in total. Contents not qualifying as Übersiedlungsgut would also attract VAT.
- A Beiladung firm handles all of this; parcels or train shift the paperwork to Tim and Michelle.

### Gaps
- No official BAZG or Swiss Post text describes how to clear Übersiedlungsgut arriving by DHL/Swiss Post parcel: enclose 18.44, upload via "value clarification", or present at a customs office. Nor whether the CHF 13 + 3% fee still applies when the goods are duty-free.
  - Recommended: call Swiss Post customs (+41 848 48 48 47) and the BAZG customs office for Zürich or the border crossing before shipping.
- Whether parcels sent **before** the van crossing can be treated as Nachsendungen is not confirmed.
