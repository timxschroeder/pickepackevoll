# Towing a light trailer (≤750 kg) behind the rented 3.5 t van, Hamburg → Zürich, class B only

Researched 2026-10-14. Most rental-company facts come from forums and help-centre snippets because the T&C pages blocked fetching (Sixt 403, mietwagen-talk 403). Unverified items are marked **[unverified]**.

## 1. Licence: may a class B holder tow ≤750 kg behind a 3.5 t van, in DE and CH?

### Takeaway
Yes. Under FeV §6, class B covers a vehicle of up to 3,500 kg zGG plus a trailer of up to 750 kg zGG, and the combined weight does not matter. Switzerland's category B has the same rule. A German class B licence is valid in Switzerland for 12 months after moving there, so a single moving trip is legal for both drivers with no BE or B96.

### Cited Findings
- FeV §6 Klasse B: motor vehicles of up to 3,500 kg zGG "(auch mit Anhänger mit einer zulässigen Gesamtmasse von nicht mehr als 750 kg oder mit Anhänger über 750 kg zulässiger Gesamtmasse, sofern 3 500 kg zulässige Gesamtmasse der Kombination nicht überschritten wird)". The 3,500 kg combination limit applies only to trailers **over** 750 kg. — [gesetze-im-internet.de FeV §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__6.html)
- BE covers class B towing vehicles with trailers of up to 3,500 kg (not needed here). — [FeV §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__6.html)
- Swiss category B: a trailer of up to 750 kg Gesamtgewicht is allowed. A heavier trailer is allowed if the Gesamtzugsgewicht stays at or below 3,500 kg (rule simplified on 1 Feb 2019). — [Kanton Aargau, Kategorie B factsheet (PDF)](https://ag.ch/media/kanton-aargau/dvi/dokumente/stva/fuehrerausweise/ausweiskategorien/kategorie-b-03072024.pdf); [Kanton Appenzell I.Rh., Kat B (PDF)](https://www.ai.ch/themen/mobilitaet-und-verkehr/strassenverkehr/fuehrerausweise/fuehrerausweiskategorien/kategorien-1/Kat-B.pdf)
- A foreign licence must be exchanged once the holder has lived in Switzerland for 12 months (Art. 42 Abs. 3bis lit. a VZV). Until then it may be used. — [Kanton Schwyz, Umtausch ausländischer Führerausweise (PDF)](https://www.sz.ch/public/upload/assets/31620/Umtausch_auslaendischer_Fuehrerausweise.pdf?fp=12)

### Inferences
- The overload problem is on the **van itself**. A trailer only helps if goods actually leave the van. Towing does not raise the van's own 3.5 t limit or its axle limits.
- The combination is about 3.5 t + 0.75 t = 4.25 t, which is legal for class B because the trailer is ≤750 kg zGG. The trailer's **registered** zGG has to be ≤750 kg. What it actually weighs on the day does not change the licence question.

### Gaps
- No ASTRA page found that states outright that an EU class B holder may tow ≤750 kg in CH. This follows from mutual recognition of categories, and the cantonal factsheets describe the Swiss category B content. No source contradicts it.

## 2. Rental vans with a tow hitch (AHK): availability, T&Cs, and how much payload the trailer really adds

### Takeaway
Sixt and Europcar let you book a tow hitch as an option. Availability depends on the vehicle group and station, and it is often "on request" for vans. Using it abroad means following the general cross-border rules (declare the country, possibly pay a fee). The drawbar load (Stützlast) rests on the van and comes out of its 815 kg payload, and so does the hitch's own weight if it is not already in the stated payload. A small unbraked box trailer adds roughly 450–550 kg of capacity. Its net effect on the van is the goods moved into the trailer minus about 50–75 kg of drawbar load **[unverified typical values]**.

### Cited Findings
- Sixt: "Anhängerkupplung" has to be added as an option when booking. Sixt says it guarantees a vehicle with a hitch at every station in Germany, "Lediglich um den Anhänger selbst, müssen Sie sich kümmern". The option only appears if the model has a hitch. Towing capacity varies by station. — [Sixt Help Center, Mietwagen mit Anhängerkupplung](https://www.sixt.de/help-center/app/articles/mietwagen-mit-anhaengerkupplung/) (seen in search snippets; the page itself returned 403); [Sixt Magazin, Mietwagen mit AHK](https://www.sixt.de/magazine/news/unsere-aktuellen-sixt-mietwagen-mit-anhaengerkupplung/)
- Forum reports: the Sixt hitch guarantee costs extra and is only available for some groups and stations. Vans "haben ja auch manchmal eine Anhängerkupplung", often with few free km. — [mietwagen-talk: AHK bei Sixt garantiert buchen](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/21503-anh-ngerkupplung-bei-sixt-garantiert-buchen/); [mietwagen-talk: Anhängerkupplung bei Mietwagen?](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/962-anh-ngerkupplung-bei-mietwagen/?pageNo=7) **[forum, unverified]**
- Europcar: the hitch can be booked as an extra, but forum users say it is non-binding and "on request". For vans, towing capacities of about 2.0–2.5 t are mentioned. — [mietwagen-talk thread 962, p.5](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/962-anh-ngerkupplung-bei-mietwagen/?pageNo=5) **[forum]**
- Europcar cross-border trips must be declared at booking or before crossing the border. The fee was quoted at €7.50/day, max €30 (possibly outdated). — [mietwagen-talk lexicon: cross border fee](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/lexicon/entry/533-cross-border-fee/) **[unverified current fee]**
- Hertz: a forum thread exists on Hertz vans with a hitch. Not fetched. — [mietwagen-talk: Hertz Transporter mit AHK](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/3680-hertz-transporter-mit-anh-ngerkupplung/)
- Crafter hitch data (towing capacity, drawbar load, gross combination weight) varies by model year and engine. What counts is what is entered in the Zulassungsbescheinigung or operating manual. Crafter vans are often listed with "AHK 3,5 t". — [carwiki.de, VW Crafter AHK](https://carwiki.de/vw-crafter-anhaengerkupplung-nachruesten)
- Unbraked small trailers: for the German Tempo-100 rule, the trailer mass may be at most 0.3 × the towing vehicle's empty mass (relevant to the trailer, not to the licence). — [9. StVOAusnV §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvoausnv_9/__1.html)

### Inferences
- **Payload arithmetic (illustrative):** move 200 kg of goods (boxes, bikes) into a trailer with about 60 kg drawbar load. The van carries 200 − 60 = **~140 kg less**. If the van's stated 815 kg payload does **not** already include a fitted hitch (~25–40 kg **[unverified]**), the net gain shrinks to about 100–115 kg. That would roughly cover the 35–115 kg overload. Adding passengers would make it tight.
- To get the most relief, load the trailer so the drawbar load sits at the legal minimum (roughly 4% of trailer mass, or 25 kg, is commonly cited **[unverified]**) and not at the maximum, while keeping it stable. Heavy items go over the trailer axle.
- The drawbar load sits behind the rear axle. Rear-axle load on a loaded box van is often the binding limit, so check it as well as gross weight.
- Rental T&Cs: none of the sources show a ban on towing with a rental van that has a hitch. Liability and insurance for **trailer** damage and theft are normally not covered by the van rental **[unverified, check the T&C text]**.

### Gaps
- No primary T&C text for Sixt, Europcar, Hertz, Buchbinder or Starcar on towing with vans, or on towing abroad and into CH. Pages blocked or not found. Ask the station in writing: is a hitch fitted on the Crafter group, is towing into CH allowed, and does the stated payload include the hitch.
- No verified figures for typical drawbar load or hitch weight on the Crafter (the 50–75 kg range is a common rule of thumb, not sourced).

## 3. Getting a trailer for a one-way trip Hamburg → Switzerland (rent, one-way, or buy and sell)

### Takeaway
No source found offers a one-way trailer rental from Germany to Switzerland. The trailer one-way market inside Germany is tiny: only Hansetrail is named, Humbaur Rent does it only on request, and there is no evidence for CH. A trailer rented in Hamburg has to go back to Hamburg, which is impractical after a one-way van trip. Buying a used trailer and selling it in CH runs into import duty/VAT and Swiss registration, because a freshly bought trailer is not Übersiedlungsgut. Buying and later reselling in Germany means a second cross-border trip.

### Cited Findings
- A forum thread asks for alternatives to Hansetrail for one-way trailer rental. A user doubts there are any: "Ist jetzt nicht der riesengroße Absatz-Markt". Humbaur Rent showed nothing on one-way and would have to be asked. — [mietwagen-talk: Anhänger Einwegmieten](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/4454-anh-nger-einwegmieten/) **[forum; date and current status of Hansetrail unknown]**
- Sixt explicitly does not supply the trailer itself, only the hitch. — [Sixt Help Center](https://www.sixt.de/help-center/app/articles/mietwagen-mit-anhaengerkupplung/)
- Hamburg trailer rental prices (round trip): NPG Moorfleeter Anhängervermietung, closed box trailer at €45/day Mon–Fri and €50/day at weekends. Elsewhere, a 750 kg trailer costs €25/weekday, €50/weekend, €90/week, €100 deposit (Braunschweig). — [Kleinanzeigen: NPG](https://www.kleinanzeigen.de/pro/npg); [Kleinanzeigen: Leihkraft-bs](https://www.kleinanzeigen.de/pro/Leihkraft-bs) **[listings, may change]**
- Swiss Übersiedlungsgut: duty-free import requires that goods "während mindestens 6 Monaten von Ihnen persönlich gebraucht worden sein" and that they keep being used after import. Vehicles fall under the general Übersiedlungsgut rules. — [BAZG: Umzug, Übersiedlungsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/umzug-uebersiedlungsgut)
- Vehicles must be cleared at the first border crossing at a staffed customs office with form 18.44. If the conditions are not met, form 15.30 may allow up to 2 years of duty-free use since moving residence, but Swiss plates are needed after at most 1 year. — [BAZG: Umzug, Fahrzeuge](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/umzug-fahrzeuge)
- ADAC: vehicles must have been registered to the person moving in Germany for at least 6 months. — [ADAC: Kfz-Mitnahme in die Schweiz](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/auto-kaufen-verkaufen/import-export/fahrzeugexport-schweiz/)

### Inferences
- **Buy-and-sell option:** a trailer bought shortly before the move **is not Übersiedlungsgut**. Selling it in CH means a proper import (duty + 8.1% VAT, Swiss registration/MFK) and selling at Swiss prices. Alternatively, keep it on German plates under form 15.30 and later drive it back to a German border town (Konstanz, Lörrach, Weil am Rhein) to sell. That costs an extra trip and paperwork. Germany also requires the trailer to be registered and insured (own plate, own liability insurance) **[general knowledge, not sourced here]**.
- **Rent in Hamburg and return it:** only works if someone drives back to Hamburg (about 850 km each way), and the van is one-way to Zürich. Not practical.
- **Swiss-side rental:** not useful, because the trailer is needed from Hamburg.
- Cost estimate for buying: a used 750 kg box or tarp trailer in DE typically costs a few hundred to about €1,500, plus registration, insurance, and the resale loss **[unverified, no source fetched]**.

### Gaps
- No current price or availability for one-way trailers DE → CH. Hansetrail's current offer could not be checked.
- No source on whether German Kurzzeitkennzeichen or Ausfuhrkennzeichen would be a practical way to move a just-bought trailer into CH.
- Customs duty rate for trailers into CH not researched.

## 4. On-road rules with a trailer: speed limits, Swiss vignette, customs for the goods

### Takeaway
Germany: 80 km/h, or 100 km/h on the Autobahn only with a Tempo-100 sticker and an approved trailer. Switzerland: 80 km/h on ordinary roads and **100 km/h on motorways** for light motor vehicles with a trailer ≤3.5 t. The trailer needs its **own Swiss vignette** (CHF 40, annual only). The e-vignette is tied to the trailer's plate. Goods in the trailer are declared as Übersiedlungsgut together with the van load.

### Cited Findings
- DE: vans and trucks up to 3.5 t with a trailer are limited to 80 km/h outside towns (§3 Abs. 3 StVO). — [StVO §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)
- DE: 100 km/h on the Autobahn and Kraftfahrstraßen also for "sonstige mehrspurige Kraftfahrzeuge bis 3,5 t mit Anhänger". This requires ABS, a Tempo-100 sticker on the trailer, the trailer's 100 km/h suitability entered in its papers, and mass ratios (unbraked trailer ≤0.3 × empty mass of the towing vehicle). — [9. StVOAusnV §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvoausnv_9/__1.html)
- CH: 80 km/h for "Anhängerzüge" (Art. 5 Abs. 1 VRV). On motorways and expressways 100 km/h for "leichte Motorwagen mit Anhänger, sofern das Gesamtgewicht des Anhängers 3,5 t nicht übersteigt" (Art. 5 Abs. 2 VRV). Older police notices still say 80 km/h on motorways (pre-2021). — [Art. 5 VRV via justement.ch](https://justement.ch/ref/5+VRV)
- CH vignette: the e-vignette is linked to the licence plate. In the webshop you choose a vehicle category that includes "Anhänger". — [BAZG FAQ Vignette](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/faq-vignette-und-e-vignette-erwerb); [BAZG: Autobahnvignette, zwei Arten](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/schweizer-autobahnvignette-gebuehr-zwei-arten)
- Trailers need their own vignette. Annual vignette only, CHF 40, valid 1 Dec of the previous year to 31 Jan of the following year. — [ÖAMTC: Maut & Vignette Schweiz](https://www.oeamtc.at/laenderinfo/schweiz/maut-vignette-in-der-schweiz-85716051) (secondary)
- Driving without a valid vignette: fine up to CHF 200 — [AXA blog: e-Vignette](https://www.axa.ch/de/privatkunden/blog/unterwegs/reisen-und-freizeit/e-vignette.html) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Extra running costs: CHF 40 trailer vignette, plus somewhat higher fuel use. A trailer with no Tempo-100 approval means 80 km/h on the whole German stretch (~800 km), which adds roughly 1–1.5 h compared with 100 km/h **[estimate]**.
- Customs: the goods in the trailer are household goods, so they go on the same 18.44 list. The trailer itself has to be declared. A rented German trailer would be temporary import (it does not stay in CH), but a one-way rental doesn't exist anyway. A bought trailer goes through the BAZG vehicle rules (section 3).
- A German-plated trailer cannot use a car's vignette. Buy a separate e-vignette for the trailer's plate before reaching the motorway in CH.

### Gaps
- Not checked whether the Basel (A2) route from the border to Zürich requires motorway use (it does in practice), so the vignette is effectively mandatory.
- No primary BAZG sentence found that literally says "Anhänger brauchen eine eigene Vignette". It follows from the plate binding plus the trailer category in the shop, and secondary sources agree.

## 5. Overall verdict: is it worth the effort?

### Takeaway
It is legal with class B, but the trailer has to come from somewhere. With no one-way trailer rental DE → CH, the only realistic way is to buy, register and insure a trailer, then deal with Swiss import or a resale trip. For a net load relief of only about 100–150 kg (after drawbar load and hitch weight), that is poor value. Lighter options are better: send boxes by parcel or freight, or use a second vehicle. Towing only makes sense if a friend or family member can lend a German-registered 750 kg trailer and someone will drive it back later, or if the rental station confirms a fitted hitch and a one-way trailer turns up.

### Cited Findings
- No one-way trailer rental to CH found. Market described as tiny. — [mietwagen-talk: Anhänger Einwegmieten](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/4454-anh-nger-einwegmieten/)
- A bought trailer misses the 6-month Übersiedlungsgut rule. — [BAZG Übersiedlungsgut](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/umzug-uebersiedlungsgut); [ADAC](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/auto-kaufen-verkaufen/import-export/fahrzeugexport-schweiz/)
- Hitch on rental vans is not guaranteed (on request, group- and station-dependent). — [mietwagen-talk thread 962](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/962-anh-ngerkupplung-bei-mietwagen/?pageNo=5)

### Inferences
- **Pitfall checklist if they still do it:** (1) get written confirmation that the rental Crafter has a hitch, may tow abroad, and what the payload is with the hitch fitted; (2) the trailer's registered zGG is ≤750 kg; (3) weigh the van **with the trailer attached**, since the drawbar load counts on the van; (4) separate CH e-vignette for the trailer's plate; (5) declare the trailer at a staffed BAZG office at the border; (6) 80 km/h in DE unless the Tempo-100 setup is in place, 100 km/h on Swiss motorways; (7) insurance and liability for a borrowed trailer.
- **Best case (borrowed German trailer, returned later by its owner):** cost about CHF 40 vignette plus fuel. Gain about 100–150 kg net off the van, which covers the stated 35–115 kg overload.

- Used-trailer market prices not sourced.
