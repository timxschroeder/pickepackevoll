# Second vehicle Hamburg → Zürich (Meilen) vs. reducing the load

Research date: 2026-10-14. Rental booking engines (sixt.de help centre, mietwagen-talk.de threads) returned 403 to the fetch tool, so some statements rest on search-result summaries of those pages and are marked "(snippet only)". No live quote for a specific date was obtained. Every euro amount for the international one-way fee is an indication, not a quote.

Project facts used (from repo data, not web): van payload 815 kg, overload ~35–115 kg, heaviest removable items in `data/items.json` are the glass cabinet (Vitrine Unterteil 70 kg + Oberteil 50 kg = 120 kg), 30 moving boxes at 10 kg (300 kg), 2 box-spring bases at 38 kg, the dining-table top 30 kg, the electric fireplace 20 kg and 2 plants at 20 kg.

## 1. One-way international rental Germany → Switzerland: allowed? Fee? Price examples?

### Takeaway
It is allowed at Sixt (with the paid "Auslandsfahrt" option) and is possible by request at Avis, Hertz and Europcar. Switzerland is the expensive case: the car cannot be re-rented in CH (non-EU), so it has to be driven back. Reported fees run from ~250 € up to ~500–700 €, on top of the daily rate and a cross-border fee. I found no published fixed tariff for Hamburg → Zürich and no confirmed live quote.

### Cited Findings
- Sixt DE: a one-way return abroad is possible in "almost all EU countries and Switzerland", but the "Auslandsfahrt" option must be booked. The one-way fee depends on distance and country, is set by the **return** country, and is shown before booking confirmation. A separate "Rückholgebühr" (recovery fee) can be added. (snippet only) — [Sixt Help Center: Einwegmiete](https://www.sixt.de/help-center/articles/wie-funktioniert-eine-einwegmiete-bei-sixt-in-deutschland/); [Sixt Einwegmiete](https://www.sixt.de/einwegmiete/)
- Sixt (international site): the one-way fee "varies by route and vehicle category". A separate travel-abroad fee applies regardless of the return location, and in a one-way rental it comes **in addition** to the one-way fee. The Sixt CH page says one-way rentals are possible with prior approval. (snippet only) — [Sixt One Way Europe](https://www.sixt.com/rental-services/europe-car-rental/one-way-europe/); [Sixt Car Rental Switzerland](https://www.sixt.com/car-rental/switzerland/)
- Mietwagen-Radar (comparison blog, updated 03.08.2026): Switzerland is a special case because a car returned there "cannot be re-rented for tax reasons" and must be physically returned. It gives Singen → Zürich as an example with fees "over 250 €", a general range of "250+ €" for Switzerland, and reports a Sixt case over 1,500 € for Italy → Germany. It suggests returning the car in a German border city and taking the train onward, with a domestic fee of ~45–100 €. — [mietwagen-radar.de Einwegmiete](https://www.mietwagen-radar.de/einwegmiete-mietwagen/)
- Same source, domestic German one-way fees: Europcar a flat 45 € for cars regardless of distance; Sixt ~45 € for long routes plus a 19.99 € base fee; Hertz from 39 €; Enterprise from 26.99 €. Commercial vehicles are "often much more expensive" for one-way. — [mietwagen-radar.de](https://www.mietwagen-radar.de/einwegmiete-mietwagen/)
- Forum/traveller reports (not verified): Europcar quoted "nearly $700 extra" for Germany → Switzerland versus ~$50 domestic, and a German forum user said the DE → CH fee can reach "up to 500 €" depending on the stations. — [FlyerTalk: Rent in Germany and return in Switzerland?](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/22367457-post2.html); [mietwagen-talk.de: One-Way International Sixt/EC](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/741-one-way-international-sixt-ec/) (snippet only)
- Hertz: a cross-border right from Germany cost 47.60 € at Berlin Tegel (older, not Zürich-specific). (snippet only) — [gemut.com Hertz one-way fee Germany](https://gemut.com/car-rental-germany/hertz-one-way-car-rental-fee-in-germany/)
- Avis CH: one-way rentals abroad must be requested by phone (0848 81 18 18). Quoted one-way prices include the one-way fee, which varies by country. — [Avis CH Einwegmiete](https://www.avis.ch/rund-um-avis/serviceleistungen/einwegmiete)
- Vans: billiger-mietwagen.de says it is "generally not possible" to return a Transporter in another country, while a forum user says Europcar does allow DE ↔ CH one-way. These sources conflict. (snippet only) — [billiger-mietwagen.de Transporter Schweiz](https://www.billiger-mietwagen.de/transporter-mieten-schweiz.html); [mietwagen-talk.de Umzug Schweiz–Deutschland](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/4280-umzug-schweiz-nach-deutschland-einwegmiete/)
- Movacar (1 € transfer rentals, used by Europcar DE since 2021): Germany-only relocations. Minimum age 25. Nothing found for routes into Switzerland. — [Europcar/Movacar press release (PDF)](https://www.presseportal.de/en/download/document/615c306f27000090e2d689b8-211005-pressemitteil-uropcar-movacar.pdf); [mietwagen-talk.de article on Movacar](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/article/309-mietwagen-fast-kostenlos-berf-hren-movacar-starcar-europcar-flipcar/)
- Capacity: an estate car carries 300 kg by weight easily. VW Passat Variant: about 510–560 kg payload (computed from kerb weight and GVW). Skoda Octavia Combi: ~510 kg. Driver and passengers count against the payload. — [autotijd.be Passat Variant weights](https://autotijd.be/de/gewicht/volkswagen/passat-variant); [kofferraum.org Octavia Combi](https://kofferraum.org/masse/skoda/octavia-combi/)

### Inferences
- **Rough cost of a car one-way Hamburg → Zürich, 1–2 days: ~450–1,000 €.** That is about 250–700 € international one-way fee + cross-border option (~20–50 €) + daily rate for an estate car. The daily rate was not verified; I assume ~60–150 €/day. Treat this as an order of magnitude until a live quote is pulled for the actual date.
- A small van (Caddy/Transporter) one-way into CH is less likely to be offered at all, and dearer if it is. An estate car is the realistic choice. With Michelle (~60–70 kg) aboard, a Kombi still has ~400+ kg for goods, which covers 100–300 kg.
- Booking path: enter Hamburg pickup and Zürich (airport or city) return in the Sixt/Europcar/Hertz booking engines and tick the foreign-travel option. Avis CH needs a phone request.

### Gaps
- No actual live quote for Hamburg → Zürich (blocked booking engines). Live quotes are needed from Sixt, Europcar, Hertz, Avis and check24/billiger-mietwagen for the move date.
- Buchbinder/Global: no information found on DE → CH one-way.
- Enterprise: international one-way policy into CH not found.

## 2. Alternative: domestic one-way to the German border (Lörrach / Weil am Rhein / Freiburg / Konstanz) plus a hop across

### Takeaway
A domestic one-way Hamburg → Lörrach costs only ~45–65 € in one-way fees. But the goods still have to cross the border. If they are moved into the van at the border, the van is overloaded again in Switzerland, so this only helps if the second car itself crosses. Workable variants: (a) the German rental crosses briefly to Basel and returns to Lörrach (not a one-way into CH), with the goods unloaded into a Swiss car, or (b) the van makes a second short Basel shuttle.

### Cited Findings
- Sixt has a station in Lörrach (Gewerbestraße 6, 79539) with 24-hour return, and one-way rentals there are possible "with a fee". (snippet only) — [Sixt Lörrach](https://www.sixt.de/mietwagen/deutschland/loerrach/)
- Europcar is listed in Lörrach, Schwarzwaldstr. 53 (directory listing, not official). — [golocal Lörrach](https://www.golocal.de/loerrach/automobile/)
- Avis CH charges no one-way fee between Basel, Bern, Geneva, Lausanne and Zürich, depending on rate type. Whether this covers vans is unclear. — [Avis CH Einwegmiete](https://www.avis.ch/rund-um-avis/serviceleistungen/einwegmiete)
- Mietwagen-Radar suggests the border-city return plus train trick (e.g. Konstanz) as the standard way to avoid the CH one-way fee. — [mietwagen-radar.de](https://www.mietwagen-radar.de/einwegmiete-mietwagen/)
- Sixt: foreign trips require the paid foreign-travel option. Without it, the insurance is void (see sibling note `Border weight split Zürich move/rental_policies.md`). — [Sixt Help Center (snippet)](https://www.sixt.de/help-center/sections/ruckgabeort-andern-und-einwegmiete/)

### Inferences
- **Variant A, "car across the border, then a Swiss car to Zürich":**
  1. Michelle drives the German rental Hamburg → Basel with 100–300 kg. That is legal, because the van is under the limit the whole way.
  2. Both vehicles clear customs together (form 18.44, one inventory).
  3. In Basel, the goods move into an Avis CH estate car (Basel → Zürich one-way, likely free).
  4. Michelle returns the German car to Lörrach (~10 km) and then takes the Swiss car.

  This takes a lot of logistics: two rentals, a transfer, return trips, and border opening hours. The saving against a direct international one-way is maybe 200–600 €, at the cost of 2–3 extra hours.
- **Variant B, Lörrach return + van shuttle:** the second car carries the excess to Lörrach and is returned. Tim's van goes Basel → Meilen legally, comes back for the rest (~2 × 85 km), and returns. This is the "Basel split" already covered in the earlier report, and it makes the second car only partly useful (it solves the German leg only).
- A domestic one-way to Freiburg/Konstanz with the goods transferred there does **not** solve the Swiss leg on its own.

### Gaps
- No Sixt/Europcar station confirmed in Weil am Rhein itself. Lörrach is confirmed for Sixt (snippet only).
- The exact domestic fee and car price Hamburg → Lörrach for the move date was not obtained.

## 3. Customs/residence: may Michelle drive a German one-way rental into CH and drop it there?

### Takeaway
While Michelle's domicile is still in Germany, she counts as a non-resident. She may drive a foreign car into CH for private use without formalities (up to 6 months a year). If she is already domiciled in Switzerland, a car rented abroad needs a Vormerkschein 15.25 at the border. That form is valid 8 days and expects the car to be taken back out or returned to a **Swiss-based** rental company. A German-registered car dropped at a Swiss station of the same brand is not clearly covered, so BAZG should be asked. Simplest rule: drive the rental across before the Swiss registration date, or make sure the drop station is the rental company's Swiss branch.

### Cited Findings
- BAZG: people domiciled abroad may use an uncleared foreign car "nur während einer beschränkten Zeit und nur für private Zwecke" (only for a limited time and only for private purposes). For cars, up to 6 months in 12 months, entry and exit need no customs formalities. The car must be registered abroad, and the vignette must be paid. — [BAZG: Unverzollte Fahrzeuge vorübergehend in der Schweiz benutzen](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/unverzollte-fahrzeuge-nutzung-schweiz-regeln)
- BAZG: people domiciled in Switzerland may in principle not use an uncleared vehicle. Rentals from abroad are an exception. At the border you request a **Vormerkschein 15.25**, valid **8 days** from the start of the rental contract (3 days if you enter more than 5 days after the rental began). The vehicle must be re-exported or returned to a rental company **based in Switzerland**. The rental contract must be carried. For other cases, the BAZG info line is +41 58 467 15 15. — [BAZG: Unverzollte Fahrzeuge](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/unverzollte-fahrzeuge-nutzung-schweiz-regeln)
- BAZG leaflet 15.49: Swiss residents may in principle not use foreign-registered vehicles, including cars lent by relatives or friends. Uncleared vehicles must be declared at the border "unverzüglich und unaufgefordert" (immediately and without being asked). (snippet only) — [BAZG Merkblatt 15.49 (PDF)](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/xG5rXtRXNkO2/mit_fahrzeugen_durchdenschweizerzollform1549.pdf)
- Forum claim (unverified): Swiss-licence holders may not enter CH with a car rented in Germany. This contradicts the BAZG 15.25 route and is likely wrong or outdated. (snippet only) — [mietwagen-talk.de Umzug Schweiz–Deutschland](https://www.mietwagen-talk.de/forum/thread/4280-umzug-schweiz-nach-deutschland-einwegmiete/)

### Inferences
- On a moving day, Michelle is normally still DE-domiciled (the Swiss Anmeldung happens after arrival), so the non-resident rule applies. The rental is a temporary import and the rental company drives it out again. Low risk.
- If she has already registered in Zürich: request a 15.25 at a staffed crossing during opening hours. A same-day drop at Sixt/Europcar Zürich (a Swiss branch) most likely satisfies "return to a CH-based rental company", but this is not explicitly confirmed. Ask BAZG.
- The goods in the second car are household goods (Übersiedlungsgut). They must be declared on form 18.44, ideally in convoy with the van as one declaration (see `customs_and_law.md`). The vehicle question and the goods question are separate.

### Gaps
- No BAZG text explicitly covers "foreign one-way rental dropped at the same brand's Swiss station" for a Swiss resident.

## 4. Own car option

### Takeaway
Whether Tim or Michelle own a car is unknown. If they do, driving it to Zürich is by far the cheapest second vehicle: just fuel, vignette and possibly import. The car itself becomes a customs/import topic once they live in CH.

### Cited Findings
- A vehicle of people moving in can be imported duty-free as Übersiedlungsgut if it was used abroad for at least 6 months before the move (it then needs Swiss registration). (snippet only) — [BAZG FAQ Fahrzeugverzollung](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/fahrzeugverzollung-schweiz-haeufige-fragen)
- Non-residents may use their foreign car in CH up to 6 months per 12 months without formalities. — [BAZG: Unverzollte Fahrzeuge](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/unverzollte-fahrzeuge-nutzung-schweiz-regeln)

### Inferences
- If they own a car they are moving anyway, it solves the overload for free. 100–300 kg in the boot is well within a typical car payload. Swiss registration and import follow the normal process.

### Gaps
- Car ownership: ask Tim.

## 5. Running cost and time of the second vehicle

### Takeaway
Hamburg → Zürich is ~880 km and 8–9 h of driving. Fuel for an estate car is ~100 €, the Swiss vignette 40 CHF if the rental lacks one. The second driver is not extra effort if Michelle travels anyway, but it does mean no swapping drivers in the van.

### Cited Findings
- Hamburg → Zürich by road: ~863–903 km, ~7 h 46 min plus breaks (route calculator), plus ~42 € tolls/vignette. — [nakordoni.eu Zürich–Hamburg](https://nakordoni.eu/ro/driving/zurich-to-hamburg)
- Swiss motorway vignette 2026: 40 CHF (sticker or e-vignette, linked to the plate), valid 1 Dec 2025 – 31 Jan 2027, no short-term version. — [Blick: neue Autobahnvignette](https://www.blick.ch/auto/service/ab-1-februar-brauchst-du-sie-10-wichtige-fakten-zur-neuen-autobahnvignette-id21647464.html); [iamexpat e-vignette](https://www.iamexpat.ch/expat-info/driving-switzerland/e-vignette)
- Swiss rentals usually include the vignette. German rentals do not, unless stated. (traveller report, snippet only) — [FlyerTalk](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/22367457-post2.html)

### Inferences
- Fuel: about 880 km × ~6.5 l/100 km ≈ 57 l × ~1.70–1.85 €/l ≈ **95–105 €**. The consumption and price are assumptions, not sourced.
- The van has a lower speed and longer breaks, so a convoy travels at van pace. Both arrive together for customs, which is good for one 18.44 declaration.
- The cost Michelle would have had anyway (train ticket Hamburg → Zürich, or riding in the van) is saved or offset. A train ticket was not researched.

### Gaps
- Fuel price on the move date. Train fare as an offset.

## 6. Comparison: reducing the load by ~100–150 kg

### Takeaway
Removing the glass cabinet alone (120 kg) covers most or all of the 35–115 kg overload. Removing 4–12 heavy boxes (books) covers the rest. Used furniture typically resells at 20–50 % of the new price, and glass cabinets are slow sellers. Selling it and re-buying in CH likely costs a few hundred euros in value loss, but zero rental logistics and zero legal grey zones.

### Cited Findings
- Used furniture generally trades at 20–50 % of the new price. (affiliate-linked guide, about buying) — [monsterdealz Gebrauchte Möbel 2026](https://www.monsterdealz.de/magazin/gebrauchte-moebel-kaufen)
- Swiss guide: furniture loses about a quarter of its value in the first year. Classics and designer pieces hold value better, and a "realistic price" is key to selling. — [Blick: Secondhand-Möbel verkaufen](https://www.blick.ch/life/wohnen/tipps-um-erfolgreich-zu-verkaufen-so-verkauft-man-seine-secondhand-moebel-am-besten-id19649948.html)
- Asking prices for used display cabinets on eBay range widely (e.g. ~120 € to 850 € for a period piece). These are asking, not sale, prices. — [eBay Stilmöbel-Vitrinen](https://www.ebay.de/b/Vitrine-Alt-in-Stilmobel-Vitrinen-Ab-1945/261258/bn_7005165673)

### Inferences
- **Reduction cost ≈ value loss of the items removed** (new price minus sale proceeds, plus re-buy cost in CH, where furniture is usually dearer). For a mid-range cabinet that may be ~200–600 €, which is similar to or below the second-vehicle cost. If the cabinet isn't loved, giving it away (Kleinanzeigen "zu verschenken") costs only its residual value.
- Low-effort hybrid: remove the 120 kg cabinet, which nearly or fully closes the gap. Then use the planner's buffer to drop a few boxes or the 20 kg electric fireplace if the van is still >815 kg.
- Ranking for "legal, cheap, low effort":
  1. Own car, if they have one.
  2. Reduce the load (sell or give away the cabinet, maybe a few boxes).
  3. Basel split / shuttle (covered in the earlier report).
  4. German rental one-way into CH: simple but ~450–1,000 €.
  5. Domestic one-way to Lörrach plus a Swiss car: cheap fees but the most complex.

### Gaps
- New price, brand and condition of the cabinet (needed for a real value-loss estimate).
- Shipping 5–10 boxes by parcel to CH (DHL/Post, plus customs as Übersiedlungsgut follow-up shipment) was not researched. It could be a cheap middle path for books.
